# Experiment Design

## Question

How do the three allocation policies behave on identical synthetic ward demand under a short admissions surge, in terms of unmet demand, average bed utilization, staffing balance, and solver execution time?

## Protocol

- Use fixed seeds `11`, `23`, and `42`; all modes for a seed/scenario receive the same deterministic arrivals. Four disruptions begin at hour 24 for up to 12 hours: 30% all-ward admissions surge, General Medicine nurse shortage, Cardiology bed closure, and emergency-department overload.
- The experiment defaults to a 72-hour horizon. Increase it with direct `run(seeds, horizon)` invocation when longer recovery windows are needed.
- Predictive allocation uses a greedy forecast allocation; Quantum Optimization uses the QUBO with local simulated annealing; Hybrid uses OR-Tools CP-SAT with forecast plus queue pressure and replans each hour.
- Save one row per seed/scenario/mode to `results/mode_comparison.csv`; create a Plotly HTML comparison chart and a grouped summary in `results/SUMMARY.md`.
- Repeatability is checked at the simulation trajectory level. Runtime is measured wall-clock time and is inherently noisier than operational metrics.

## Outcomes and Interpretation

The runner reports mean bed utilization, cumulative and final unmet demand, mean patients-per-nurse standard deviation, and average solver time. The sample is synthetic and small, the rules are simplified, and the number of seeds is exploratory. Results do not support clinical conclusions or claims of quantum advantage. No statistical significance claim is made.

## Data

The default dataset is generated by `scripts/generate_synthetic_data.py`, with hourly timestamps, department seasonality, Poisson noise, and two marked surge intervals. `scripts/fetch_public_data.py` optionally accepts a user-provided aggregate CSV URL through `PUBLIC_HEALTH_DATA_URL`; schema/network failures fall back to synthetic data. No external public source is bundled or treated as a verified validation set.
